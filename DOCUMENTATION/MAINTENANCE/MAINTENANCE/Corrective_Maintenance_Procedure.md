# Corrective Maintenance Procedure - MIAO-MB Model

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### Purpose
This procedure defines the requirements for responding to biomedical equipment failures, performing corrective repairs, and documenting activities to restore equipment to service while maintaining patient safety and regulatory compliance.

### Scope
This procedure applies to all unscheduled equipment maintenance activities, including:
- Equipment failures during operation
- Equipment malfunctions discovered during use
- Safety system failures or deviations
- Performance degradation

### Standards Referenced
- ISO 9001:2015 - Quality Management
- ISO 13485:2016 - Medical Device Quality Management
- ISO 14971:2019 - Risk Management

---

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Corrective Maintenance** | Unscheduled maintenance performed to restore failed or malfunctioning equipment to service |
| **Equipment Failure** | Equipment is unable to perform its intended function |
| **MTTR** | Mean Time To Repair - average time from failure detection to equipment return to service |
| **Root Cause** | Underlying reason for equipment failure or malfunction |
| **Spare Part** | Component used to replace failed part during corrective maintenance |
| **Safety-Critical Failure** | Equipment failure that poses direct risk to patient safety |
| **Non-Safety Failure** | Equipment failure that does not pose direct risk to patient safety |

---

## 3. Responsibilities

### Maintenance Director
- Determine resource allocation for urgent repairs
- Authorize emergency external support if needed
- Review MTTR performance
- Approve major repair decisions
- Escalate urgent issues to management

### Maintenance Supervisor
- Respond to failure notifications
- Assess failure severity
- Assign qualified technician
- Coordinate emergency response if needed
- Prioritize workload
- Track repair progress
- Authorize expedited repairs

### Reporting Department/Equipment User
- Detect and report equipment failure
- Stop use of failed equipment
- Provide details of symptoms
- Assist technician with access
- Implement alternative procedures
- Sign off when equipment returned

### Maintenance Technician
- Respond within required timeframe
- Diagnose equipment failure
- Perform or arrange repairs
- Document all activities
- Test equipment functionality
- Notify user of status updates

### Quality Manager
- Review corrective maintenance documentation
- Perform root cause analysis for serious failures
- Track failure trends
- Identify systemic issues
- Recommend preventive measures

## 4. Failure Detection and Initial Response

### Failure Notification

**Who Can Report:**
- Equipment users/operators
- Maintenance personnel
- Quality/safety personnel
- Any staff member

**How to Report:**
1. Contact Maintenance Supervisor immediately (or maintenance line)
2. Provide equipment name and location
3. Describe symptoms or failure mode
4. Indicate if patient safety is at risk
5. Note time of failure discovery

**Reporting Information:**
- Equipment ID and location
- Nature of failure
- Equipment status (safe/unsafe)
- Date and time discovered
- User contact information

### Severity Assessment

**Safety-Critical Failure (URGENT):**
- Patient could be harmed
- Equipment poses immediate risk
- Response required: < 1 hour
- Actions:
  1. Immediately isolate equipment
  2. Post "OUT OF SERVICE" sign
  3. Notify management
  4. Dispatch technician immediately
  5. Arrange alternative equipment

**Critical Failure (HIGH PRIORITY):**
- Essential equipment non-functional
- Response required: 2-4 hours
- Actions:
  1. Stop use of equipment
  2. Notify Maintenance Supervisor
  3. Dispatch technician
  4. Document failure details

**Important Failure (ROUTINE):**
- Non-essential equipment non-functional
- Response required: Next working day
- Actions:
  1. Document failure
  2. Notify Maintenance Supervisor
  3. Schedule technician
  4. Implement workaround if needed

**Minor Issue (LOW PRIORITY):**
- Equipment functional but degraded
- Response required: Within 1 week
- Actions:
  1. Document issue
  2. Schedule for next maintenance slot
  3. Monitor performance

---

## 5. Equipment Isolation and Safety

### Immediate Actions

**If Safety-Critical Failure:**
1. Cease all use of equipment immediately
2. Isolate equipment from power and utilities
3. Place "OUT OF SERVICE" sign on equipment
4. Prevent other staff from using equipment
5. Notify all potentially affected users

**If Moderate Failure:**
1. Stop use of failed function
2. Assess if equipment can be used for other functions
3. Document limitations
4. Use alternative equipment if available

**Safety Considerations:**
- Do not restart failed equipment without authorization
- Do not attempt repairs without proper training
- Do not use non-approved spare parts
- Follow infection control procedures
- Ensure maintenance area is safe for work

## 6. Diagnosis and Troubleshooting

### Technician Arrival and Initial Assessment

**Upon Arrival:**
1. Verify equipment identity and location
2. Greet equipment user and gather information
3. Perform visual inspection
4. Review recent maintenance history
5. Consult equipment manual if needed

### Diagnostic Process

**Step 1: Information Gathering**
- What was the equipment doing when failure occurred?
- What symptoms are present?
- When was equipment last serviced?
- Has this happened before?
- What changed recently?

**Step 2: Visual Inspection**
- Inspect for obvious damage or wear
- Check all connections and seals
- Look for leaks, discoloration, or debris
- Verify all safety covers in place
- Check indicator lights and displays

**Step 3: Power and Systems Check**
- Verify power supply is connected and functional
- Check circuit breaker or fuse status
- Verify control system responsiveness
- Test displays and gauges
- Check sensor readings

**Step 4: Functional Testing**
- Run diagnostic tests per equipment manual
- Perform any self-test functions
- Test individual components or subsystems
- Take measurements if applicable
- Document all test results

**Step 5: Root Cause Determination**
- Based on testing, identify likely cause
- Verify hypothesis with additional tests if needed
- Consult supervisor if diagnosis unclear
- Document suspected cause

## 7. Repair Options and Decision

### Repair vs. Replace Decision

**Decision Criteria:**
1. **Cost Analysis:**
   - Cost of repair
   - Cost of replacement
   - Cost comparison

2. **Time Analysis:**
   - Time to repair
   - Time to replace
   - Equipment availability impact

3. **Risk Analysis:**
   - Can equipment be safely repaired?
   - Is repair certain to resolve issue?
   - Is replaced part reliable?

4. **Strategic Decision:**
   - Is equipment near end of life?
   - Are spare parts available?
   - Is equipment still supported?

### Decision Authority:
- **Minor repairs (< €200):** Technician/Supervisor
- **Standard repairs (€200-€1000):** Supervisor approval
- **Major repairs (€1000-€5000):** Director approval
- **Replacement decision:** Director + Management approval

### Documentation:
- Decision documented in intervention report
- Rationale recorded
- Approval recorded if required

## 8. Repair Execution

### Repair Planning

**Before Starting Repair:**
1. Gather all required tools and parts
2. Verify spare parts are approved and functional
3. Document all work to be performed
4. Estimate time required
5. Plan for testing after repair

### Repair Procedures

**General Guidelines:**
1. Follow equipment manual exactly
2. Use only approved spare parts
3. Document each step performed
4. Take photos if complex repair
5. Note any difficulties or observations
6. Keep work area clean and organized

**Part Replacement:**
1. Identify exact part to be replaced
2. Obtain approved replacement part
3. Verify serial number matches
4. Record part serial number
5. Store removed part appropriately

**System Testing During Repair:**
- Test subsystems as work progresses
- Identify and address issues immediately
- Make adjustments as needed

### Equipment Re-Assembly

1. Verify all components are properly installed
2. Check all connections are secure
3. Verify all safety systems are functional
4. Confirm covers and seals in place
5. Clean all external surfaces

---

## 9. Post-Repair Testing and Verification

### Functional Testing

**Before Return to Service:**
1. Power on equipment and observe startup
2. Run full diagnostic test
3. Perform functional test per manual
4. Verify all safety systems operational
5. Measure performance against specifications
6. Test with simulated load if applicable
7. Document all test results

### Quality Verification

1. Visual inspection of repairs
2. Cleanliness verification
3. Documentation review
4. Safety system verification
5. Performance measurement

### Return to Service Approval

- Equipment meets all specifications
- All safety systems verified
- Testing results acceptable
- Documentation complete
- User trained on any changes

## 10. Documentation Requirements

### Intervention Report Must Include:
- Equipment identification (name, ID, serial number)
- Failure date and time of discovery
- Failure symptoms and description
- Technician assigned and response time
- Diagnostic findings
- Root cause determination
- Repair performed (detailed)
- Parts replaced (part numbers and serial numbers)
- Testing results before and after
- Equipment status (functional/not functional)
- Approval signatures
- Technician name and signature
- Comments and observations

### Documentation Storage:
- Original filed in equipment maintenance folder
- Copy to equipment user department
- Electronic entry in maintenance system
- Retained minimum 2 years

## 11. Root Cause Analysis

### When Required:
- Safety-critical equipment failure
- Repeated failure of same equipment
- Unexpected failure of new equipment
- Failure during preventive maintenance

### Analysis Process:

**Step 1: Gather Information**
- Interview technician who performed repair
- Review equipment manual
- Review maintenance history
- Review previous failures (if any)
- Examine failed part

**Step 2: Identify Possible Causes**
- Equipment design defect?
- Manufacturing defect?
- Inadequate maintenance?
- User error?
- Environmental factors?
- Wear and tear?

**Step 3: Analyze and Verify**
- Most likely cause?
- How do we know?
- Supporting evidence?
- Any contributing factors?

**Step 4: Determine Corrective Action**
- Can failure be prevented?
- Design change needed?
- Maintenance interval change?
- Training needed?
- Equipment replacement?

**Step 5: Document**
- Root cause analysis form
- Recommended actions
- Responsible parties
- Implementation timeline

## 12. Performance Monitoring

### Key Performance Indicators

| KPI | Formula | Target | Monitoring |
|-----|---------|--------|-----------|
| MTTR | Total repair time / Number of failures | Minimize | Monthly |
| Failure Rate | Failures / Equipment / Month | Minimize | Monthly |
| Repeat Failures | Repeated failures / Total failures | < 10% | Quarterly |
| Repair Success | First-time repairs / Total repairs | > 90% | Monthly |
| Safety Issues | Safety-critical failures | Zero target | Immediately |

### Monthly Review:
1. Calculate MTTR
2. Analyze failure trends
3. Identify repeat issues
4. Review spare parts usage
5. Report to management

## 13. Spare Parts Management

### Approved Spare Parts:
- List maintained by Maintenance Supervisor
- Only approved parts used
- Parts are documented and tracked
- Serial numbers recorded
- Expiration dates verified

### Procurement:
- Order through approved suppliers
- Verify part specifications before ordering
- Maintain inventory of common parts
- Check expiration dates upon receipt
- Store in appropriate conditions

---

## 14. External Support and Vendor Repairs

### When to Involve Vendors:
- Repair requires manufacturer authorization
- Equipment is under warranty
- Technician lacks required expertise
- Complex diagnostic equipment needed
- Parts require manufacturer programming

### Vendor Management:
1. Contact authorized service provider
2. Provide detailed failure description
3. Coordinate equipment pickup/delivery
4. Track repair status
5. Verify proper completion upon return
6. Document vendor invoice and report
7. Follow up for feedback
